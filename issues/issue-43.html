ISSUE #43 • 2026-W44 Monday, October 26, 2026 (Oct 19 – Oct 26, 2026)
THREAT LEVEL: Elevated

Vendor ACH Bank Change Scams, WinRAR/7-Zip Flaws & Fake Geek Squad Invoices

Business Email Compromise (BEC) targeting small contractors and agencies surged last week alongside a file-archiver security update. We filtered 36 corporate IT stories down to 3 essential items.

#1 Priority 5-Minute Team Action (Week #43)

Establish a strict rule: never update a vendor or contractor's ACH bank routing details based solely on an email request.

Krebs on Security Wire & ACH Fraud
Critical • ⏱ 2 mins

Scammers Hijack Contractor Email Threads to Reroute ACH Payments

Attackers who break into a supplier or freelancer's email account wait quietly until an invoice is due, then reply inside the real email thread asking you to send payment to a 'new bank account.' Because the message comes from the vendor's actual email address and quotes your real project details, spam filters cannot catch it. Once an ACH or wire payment leaves your business checking account, banks rarely recover the funds.

ACH / Wire Transfers, Bill.com, Business Email Source Advisory ↗
CISA Windows Software
High • ⏱ 4 mins

Outdated 7-Zip and WinRAR File Extraction Tools Exploited via Email Attachments

CISA alerted organizations that hackers are hiding malware inside compressed `.zip` and `.rar` archives that trigger automatically on computers running old versions of WinRAR or 7-Zip. Because Windows 11 now opens `.zip` and `.rar` files natively, most small teams no longer even need third-party archive tools installed. Removing or updating these old utilities closes an easy door for attackers.

Windows 10/11, 7-Zip, WinRAR Source Advisory ↗
BleepingComputer Phone Scams
Medium • ⏱ 1 min

Fake 'Norton / Geek Squad' $499 Renewal Emails Trick Staff Into Installing Remote Control Tools

Scammers are blasting small business inboxes with fake receipts claiming your company card was just billed $499 for an annual computer support plan. When a worried employee calls the phone number on the receipt to cancel the charge, the scammer asks them to install remote-desktop software like AnyDesk or ScreenConnect to 'process the refund' and steals bank logins. There is no charge on your card—the email is pure bait to get you on the phone.

Business Email, AnyDesk / TeamViewer Source Advisory ↗
