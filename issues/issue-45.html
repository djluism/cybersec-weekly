ISSUE #45 • 2026-W46 Monday, November 9, 2026 (Nov 2 – Nov 9, 2026)
THREAT LEVEL: Elevated

Fake QuickBooks Invoices, Chrome Zero-Day Patch & Small Office Wi-Fi Router Attacks

This week, scammers are targeting 1–5 person shops with convincing QuickBooks and Stripe payment failed emails while attackers actively exploit outdated Chrome browsers and home-office routers. We filtered out 38 enterprise data-center alerts so your team can focus on three 5-minute fixes.

#1 Priority 5-Minute Team Action (Week #45)

Update Google Chrome on all team laptops today and verify any unexpected invoice or banking change via a quick phone call before clicking.

CISA Browser & Workstations
Critical • ⏱ 2 mins

Urgent Google Chrome & Edge Update Fixes Active Web Exploit

CISA ordered federal agencies to patch a critical flaw in Google Chrome and Microsoft Edge after hackers began using booby-trapped websites to steal login cookies. If anyone on your team visits a compromised webpage without this update, attackers can hijack active email or banking sessions without needing your password. Restart your browser right now so the automatic security patch finishes installing.

Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Brave Source Advisory ↗
Krebs on Security Email & Phishing
High • ⏱ Zero cost / Awareness

Tax & Bookkeeping Phishing Wave Spoofs Intuit QuickBooks Reminders

Cybercriminals are sending realistic 'Account Suspended' and 'Unpaid Invoice' alerts that appear to come directly from QuickBooks and Stripe. Clicking the link opens a lookalike login page that captures both your password and your six-digit text message security code in real time. Never log in through an email button—always open your browser bookmark to check billing alerts.

QuickBooks Online, Stripe, Business Email Source Advisory ↗
BleepingComputer Home & Small Office Wi-Fi
High • ⏱ 5 mins

Botnet Targets Unpatched Asus, TP-Link, and Netgear Small-Office Routers

A newly discovered wave of automated attacks is hijacking small-office and home Wi-Fi routers that still have 'Remote Management' turned on or run outdated firmware. Once inside a router, criminals can quietly slow down your internet and intercept unencrypted office traffic. Rebooting your router alone does not fix the issue unless you install the latest firmware update in the router app.

TP-Link, Asus Routers, Netgear SOHO Source Advisory ↗
BleepingComputer Cloud & File Sharing
Medium • ⏱ 3 mins

Fake Google Drive & DocuSign Collaboration Invites Spread Info-Stealers

Attackers are abusing legitimate Google Drive and DocuSign notification emails to send small business owners links to fake PDF contract previews. Instead of opening a contract, the page asks you to download a '.zip' or '.msi' file that quietly steals saved passwords from your computer. Real Google Docs and DocuSign agreements view directly in your browser and never ask you to install software to read a document.

Google Workspace, DocuSign, Windows PCs Source Advisory ↗
CISA Apple Devices
High • ⏱ 5 mins

Apple Security Update Protects iPhones and Macs From Malicious Image Files

CISA added a recent Apple macOS and iOS security flaw to its must-patch list after attackers used specially crafted image files to compromise Apple devices. Because so many small teams handle customer texts, iMessages, and email attachments on personal iPhones and MacBooks, unpatched devices are easy targets. Updating to the latest iOS and macOS release completely blocks this attack.

iPhone (iOS), MacBook (macOS), iPad Source Advisory ↗
Krebs on Security Banking & Payroll
Medium • ⏱ 3 mins

Fake 'Sponsored' Google Search Ads Impersonate Small Business Banks

Scammers are buying top Google Search ad slots for regional banks and payroll platforms like Gusto and ADP to trick busy owners into logging into fake websites. These spoofed portals look identical to your real bank login and immediately trigger a wire transfer while you think your page is loading. Never use a search engine to visit your bank or payroll login page.

Business Banking, Gusto / ADP, Google Search Source Advisory ↗
