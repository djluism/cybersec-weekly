ISSUE #46 • 2026-W46 Monday, November 12, 2026 (Nov 5 – Nov 12, 2026)
THREAT LEVEL: High

Zero-Day Flaws Strike Outlook and Chrome While Notion Discloses AI Data Leak

This week's updates demand immediate attention as active exploits target everyday business tools including Outlook, Google Chrome, and Notion. We filtered out 17 complex enterprise notices regarding industrial refrigeration controllers, Linux rootkits, and telecom server vulnerabilities to focus on your day-to-day office tools.

#1 Priority 5-Minute Team Action (Week #46)

Restart your computer and open both Windows Update and your web browser to install this month's critical security patches immediately.

Microsoft Issues Massive Update Addressing Active Outlook Flaws

Microsoft has released its monthly security update fixing over a thousand vulnerabilities across Windows, including an actively exploited email bug in Outlook. Attackers can trigger this Outlook flaw without requiring the victim to open an attachment or click a malicious link. Running Windows Update now protects your workstation from unauthorized background access.

Notion AI Chat Logs Exposed Following Third-Party Vendor Breach

Notion disclosed that a cybersecurity intrusion at one of its artificial intelligence partners exposed customer conversation transcripts. If your small team used Notion AI to draft sensitive business proposals, client notes, or internal policies, those prompts may have been visible to unauthorized parties. The incident serves as a reminder to avoid pasting sensitive customer records or banking details into AI assistants.

Microsoft confirmed that this month's crucial security update is failing to install on select Windows 11 PCs, returning error messages instead. Hackers Hijacking Remote Support Software to Access Client Computers

Attackers are actively compromising outsourced IT support providers and abusing remote access tools to push malicious software onto client computers. If your business works with an external computer technician who uses remote desktop software, an intruder who breaches their firm could gain instant entry to your office PCs. Reviewing and removing old remote-access software keeps your machines shielded from third-party break-ins.
