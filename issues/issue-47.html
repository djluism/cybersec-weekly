ISSUE #47 • 2026-W47 Monday, November 18, 2026 (Nov 11 – Nov 18, 2026)
THREAT LEVEL: High

Microsoft 365 Login Traps, Hijacked Major Websites, and AI Voice Scams

This week's biggest risks for small teams come from sneaky Microsoft 365 login traps, compromised popular websites, and scammers using realistic fake voices to impersonate tech support. We filtered out 32 industrial factory alerts, developer server flaws, and corporate-only warnings so you can focus strictly on what protects your small business.

#1 Priority 5-Minute Team Action (Week #47)

Switch your Microsoft 365 and key business logins to a passwordless passkey or an authenticator app, and tell your team never to approve unexpected login prompts.

BleepingComputer Email & Phishing
Critical • ⏱ 5 mins

New Phishing Kits Steal Microsoft 365 Logins Even With Text-Message Codes

Criminals are using off-the-shelf fake login pages to intercept Microsoft 365 passwords and steal active session tokens as soon as someone signs in. Because these fake pages capture your unlocked session right after you enter a text or email code, hackers can slip straight into your business inbox and send fraudulent invoices to clients. Turning on phishing-resistant passkeys or using an authenticator app with number matching stops these fake login pages from reusing your session.

Krebs on Security Browser Security
High • ⏱ 3 mins

Hackers Used a Popular Website Tool to Slip Malware Onto Major Sites

Attackers exploited a security flaw in Optimizely, a widely used website testing service, to silently load data-stealing scripts onto at least 178 major sites including Zoom, CNN, and the BBC. Even when you only visit trusted, well-known websites during your workday, hidden third-party scripts can still try to grab saved browser data or trick you into downloading malware. Keeping your web browser updated and using a reputable ad or script blocker prevents most of these sneaky background scripts from running.

BleepingComputer Scams & Impersonation
High • ⏱ 5 mins

Scammers Are Using AI Voices and Fake Support Calls to Trick Small Teams

Cybercriminals are increasingly using AI-generated voice clones and fake tech support calls to impersonate coworkers, vendors, or software helpdesks. In a small 1-to-5 person business, one convincing phone call asking a teammate to reset a password or approve a payment can quickly drain an account or lock you out of your tools. Setting a simple rule to always verify urgent requests by calling back on a saved phone number stops these impersonation tricks cold.

Phone & SMS, Email, Accounting & Banking Source Advisory ↗
BleepingComputer Cloud Services
Medium • ⏱ 2 mins

Apple Outages Disrupt iCloud Mail and Account Sign-Ins

Apple experienced service disruptions affecting iCloud Mail, account sign-ins, and several connected Apple services. If your business uses an iCloud email address or Apple devices for daily work, sudden login errors or missing emails can look like a hack and tempt someone into clicking fake Apple Support links. Checking Apple's official status page first helps you confirm an outage safely without handing your password to opportunistic scammers.
