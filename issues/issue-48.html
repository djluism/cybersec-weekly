THREAT LEVEL: Elevated

Synology Backup Attacks, Compromised Major Websites, and AI Impersonation Scams

This week's biggest risks for small teams involve actively targeted Synology backup drives, data-stealing code hidden on major websites like Zoom, and convincing impersonation scams. We filtered out 26 enterprise-only alerts—such as industrial factory software flaws and supercomputer breaches—so you can focus strictly on what protects your business.

#1 Priority 5-Minute Team Action (Week #48)

Log into your Synology backup box today to install any pending software updates, and make sure automatic updates are turned on in your web browser.

CISA Backups & Storage
Critical • ⏱ 5 mins

Urgent Update Needed for Synology Office Backup Drives

Federal security officials warned this week that hackers are actively breaking into Synology office backup boxes through a software flaw. If your small business uses a Synology drive to store client records, invoices, or shared files, attackers could hijack it or lock you out of your backups. Installing the latest software update closes this gap right away and keeps your backup box protected.

Krebs on Security Browser & Website Security
High • ⏱ 3 mins

Major Websites Including Zoom Exposed Visitors to Hidden Data-Stealing Code

Cybercriminals slipped data-stealing software onto more than 170 major websites—including Zoom—by exploiting a popular website customization tool called Optimizely. Simply visiting an affected site or running unmonitored third-party marketing scripts on your own business website can quietly expose passwords and sensitive customer data. Keeping your web browser updated and reviewing any third-party plugins on your company site blocks these hidden traps.

Zoom, Web Browsers, Business Websites Source Advisory ↗
BleepingComputer Operating Systems
Medium • ⏱ 5 mins

Windows 11 Update Causing Microsoft Office App Launch Glitches

Microsoft confirmed that this month's Windows 11 update is causing Microsoft Office and other everyday apps to fail when installing or opening. For a small team trying to send invoices or edit documents, this glitch can bring daily work to a frustrating halt until you apply Microsoft's official workaround. Restarting affected devices and checking for the latest follow-up fix restores normal app access without risking your security protection.

Windows 11, Microsoft Office Source Advisory ↗
BleepingComputer Scams & Social Engineering
High • ⏱ 2 mins

Scammers Using Fake Voice Clones and Tech Support Calls to Steal Logins

Scammers are increasingly using realistic artificial intelligence voice clones and fake tech-support calls to trick employees into sharing passwords and account codes. In a small business of one to five people, a convincing call pretending to be your software vendor or a co-worker can quickly lead to hijacked email or bank accounts. Setting up a simple rule to verify any urgent login or payment request through a second channel stops these impersonators cold.

Business Email, Phone & SMS, Cloud Accounts Source Advisory ↗
BleepingComputer Browser Security
Medium • ⏱ 2 mins

Microsoft Edge Adds Stronger Lockdown Defenses Against Sketchy Websites

Microsoft is testing a stricter lockdown setting for its Edge web browser that turns off unnecessary graphics features often targeted by malicious websites. Since small teams spend most of their workday inside a browser handling email, banking, and cloud tools, tightening browser defenses cuts down the risk of drive-by infections. Turning on Edge's existing built-in security mode gives you strong everyday protection right now while this new feature rolls out.
