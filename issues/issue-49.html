ISSUE #49 • 2026-W48 Tuesday, November 24, 2026 (Nov 17 – Nov 24, 2026)
THREAT LEVEL: Elevated

Active Attacks Hit Synology Backup Drives, Mainstream Websites, and Phone Scams

This week's threats focus on everyday small-business risks, including active attacks on Synology office backup drives, hidden malware on popular websites, and phone scammers targeting work logins. We reviewed 26 raw security alerts and filtered out 21 corporate, industrial, and data-center stories that do not affect a 1-to-5 person team.

#1 Priority 5-Minute Team Action (Week #49)

Spend five minutes updating any Synology backup drives and office web browsers today, and remind your team never to share passwords or login codes over the phone.

CISA Backups & Storage
Critical • ⏱ 5 mins

Hackers Are Actively Targeting Unpatched Synology Backup Drives

Government cybersecurity officials confirmed this week that hackers are actively breaking into Synology office backup drives using a known software flaw. If your small business stores client files or daily backups on a Synology box connected to the internet, attackers could steal or lock up your data. Installing the latest software update closes this doorway immediately.

Krebs on Security Browser Security
High • ⏱ 3 mins

Malware Sneaks Onto Major Websites Through Popular Marketing Tool

Hackers hijacked a widely used website testing service to silently slip data-stealing software onto nearly two hundred popular sites, including Zoom and major news outlets. Simply visiting an affected page on your office computer could expose saved passwords or login sessions without you ever clicking a suspicious download. Keeping your web browser updated and using a reputable tracker blocker stops most of these sneaky scripts before they load.

Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Safari, Zoom Source Advisory ↗
BleepingComputer Phone & Login Scams
High • ⏱ 2 mins

New Extortion Gang Uses Fake Support Phone Calls to Steal Logins

A new cybercrime group is calling employees while pretending to be tech support to trick them into handing over their main work account passwords and login codes. For a small team, one fooled coworker on the phone can give criminals access to your shared email, cloud files, and customer records. Reminding your staff to hang up and verify any unexpected support request stops this scam cold.

Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Business Phone Source Advisory ↗
BleepingComputer AI & Data Privacy
Medium • ⏱ 5 mins

AI Service Drops Providers Caught Leaking User Prompts and Secrets

A popular platform that connects apps to different artificial intelligence models removed three providers after discovering they broke privacy promises and exposed user prompts and secret keys. If your business uses third-party AI writing or coding tools, anything you paste into a chat box—including client names, invoices, or passwords—could accidentally become visible to outsiders. Switching to trusted business AI plans with clear privacy guarantees and scrubbing sensitive details first keeps your company data safe.

OpenRouter, AI Writing & Coding Tools Source Advisory ↗
Krebs on Security Fraud & Scam Awareness
Medium • ⏱ Awareness

Tech Executive Charged After Fake Ransomware Rescue Firm Paid Hackers

Federal prosecutors charged a co-founder of RansomwareHelp with wire fraud after discovering the company lied about using special recovery software and instead just secretly paid hackers and charged victims a markup. Small businesses panicking after a file-locking attack are prime targets for shady rescue firms promising fast miracles. Keeping an offline backup on a disconnected drive ensures you never have to trust a middleman or pay a ransom.

External Backup Drives, Cloud Backups Source Advisory ↗
